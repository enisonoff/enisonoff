# Learnings from other creators + video types to try

What we've learned from studying reels Elena liked, and formats worth testing. Add a section each time we break down a new creator. Keep it to what's useful for her account.

## Script learnings

- **Riddle hook with a nasty answer:** "What's worse than cheating on your wife for 41 years? Doing it with a customer who happens to be a Chinese spy." (@morningbrew)
- **Fact, then a jab, every time.** Short undercuts after each fact: "So Jensen immediately continued to do business with them? What?" Two-word asides ("What?" "Huh.") keep the pace up. (@morningbrew)
- **Plant an open loop early, close it at the end.** "This story might be the first of many" at 0:06, paid off at 1:05. End on a callback, not a summary. (@morningbrew)
- **Hook types that work for explainers:** a concrete dollar figure ("$1,000 in a bank 5 years ago is worth $800 today"), a contrarian question ("what's more risky, cash or stocks? If you said stocks, you're mostly wrong"), a time promise ("it only takes two minutes"). (@matildehere)
- **Don't open with "part 2" / "lesson 2 of 8."** It tells new viewers they're mid-show; her lesson 2 was her lowest reel. Mention the series at the end. (@matildehere)
- **Withheld-answer hook, then delay the payoff:** "There are two very simple words I use to decide if something is worth posting." Names a count, hides the answer for 24 s, teases the *second* answer with proof (a stats screenshot) before revealing the first, so viewers stay for both. Each reveal: label, one-line definition, the feeling it creates, the result. Close with a one-line tool ("use this filter"). Caption: "ep. 1", ask what they want in ep. 2. (@elliewfrazier, 74 s, ~5.8K likes)
- **Characters and tension, no advice endings.** (Elena's own rule; see CLAUDE.md.)
- **No "three reasons" lists.** Elena finds numbered lists flat. Tell it as scenes (the party, the hangover, the twist) with one open loop planted in the first 5 s, teased mid-way, paid off with a callback to the hook.
- **Simple declarative sentences.** Short, plain, slightly provocative lines ("They didn't win either.") beat long explanatory ones on Instagram. Max ~4 numbers said out loud per video section; put the rest on screen or cut.
- **Hook she picked (Nov 2026):** withheld person, "There's one person who ___. It's not [the obvious one]." 

## Editing / visual learnings

- **A source card for every claim:** outlet logo, one sentence, key phrase highlighted. Builds trust on gossipy stories. (@morningbrew, @poojaduttttt)
- **Cheap one-second meme hits** about every 15 s (explosion, red arrow with "(no relation)", a sticker word like "simp"). (@morningbrew) Our versions: the Jensen clones, the billion-dollar bill.
- **Headers that land word by word with a blur-in.** (@nextbysophie, @romina.aminian) Already in the edit-video skill.
- **Big text above her head, two fonts:** italic serif lead-in + tall block caps. (@withkyndall, himani reel) Already in use.
- **Cinematic subtitle captions** for calmer, story-driven videos: Helvetica/Arial regular (free match: Arimo), small white, sentence case, full phrases, 1-2 lines, faint shadow, no outline. (@_cowmi)
- **Cover text in one plain bold font,** lowercase, phrased as a relatable line ("when u take two days off from the news..."). (@databutmakeitfashion)
- **Font trio to try:** Gloock caps + Great Vibes script + DM Mono small line (CapCut-style).

## @zauey.talks (closest to Elena's format), last ~2 months, pulled Nov 2026
Median ~106K views. Top: Nobel chemistry explained 2.46M, "70% of websites are vulnerable" 1.68M, "What exactly is Nvidia?" 1.2M, Nobel optogenetics 587K, "Who is Nvidia's biggest challenger" 356K. Bottom: Ring security robot 33K, "why the internet kept breaking" 60K.
- **Explain the thing underneath the headline.** During the Jensen scandal her hits were "what is Nvidia? (in 3 minutes)" and "what is AMD? (in 2 minutes)", not the drama. A beginner-friendly explainer of the company/tech everyone's suddenly hearing about travels far beyond followers.
- **Title card = plain question + time promise:** big lowercase white sans, 2 lines, with a small subtitle: "what is nvidia? (in 3 minutes)", "nobel prize chemistry *explained*". Sits low on her chest, on screen for the first several seconds.
- **First frame shows proof:** a headline screenshot card under the title on news-y ones (Nobel, PHP bug).
- **Stakes in a number:** "70% of the world's websites", "the last 72 hours have been absolutely insane".
- **One household analogy carries the explainer:** CPU = a few expert chefs, GPU = a thousand line cooks who only chop onions. Drawn as simple illustrated cards.
- **Visual changes every ~3 s:** paper-style cards, teal diagram panels, product photos, logos; small word-by-word captions at the top with one yellow word.
- **Product launches and incremental news underperform** (Ring robot, outages follow-up).
- Same setup as Elena: seated, one take, lav mic, 2-3 min, emoji-bullet captions. So the difference is topic choice + framing + visuals, not length or format.

## @bloombergbusiness "Jensen recap" reel (Nov 2026), the style Elena wants to use everywhere
43 s, faceless narration, 841 likes. https://www.instagram.com/reel/DgLYL2By15M/
- **One device carries the whole video:** a glowing cyan vertical timeline "spine" runs through every frame; the camera pans down it to each new stop. ~7 stops in 43 s, a new one every ~5 s.
- **Each stop:** huge white date number (bold grotesk) left of the spine, a white dot on the spine with a dotted line out, month in bold white, 1-3 short lines of gray summary text.

- **Background:** near-black navy with a vignette and faint falling streaks.
- **Charts:** one glowing mint/cyan line on the dark background, gray $ gridlines, no boxes.
- **Captions:** white, bold sans, sentence case, full phrase on 1-2 lines, lower third. No highlight box.
- Opens on 4 s of live b-roll before the first timeline stop.

## @maealicesuzuki "afraid of being uneducated" reel (Nov 2026)
59 s, ~38K likes, 159 comments. Self-improvement, but the craft transfers.
- **Hook = a contrarian rule that stings:** "You should be more afraid of being uneducated than being lonely." Then she names the reaction: "It sounds harsh, so let me explain." That buys the next 50 seconds.
- **Proof as texture:** full-screen scans of the study itself (the table and the highlighted paragraph), with her as a small cutout in the corner. The page reads as evidence even if nobody reads it.
- **Name the concept, give it a label:** "hedonic adaptation" and "mastery" pop on as little paper stickers. One jargon term per idea makes it feel smart and searchable.
- **Picture-in-picture over b-roll:** for lists ("skills, degrees, experience"), each word gets its own aesthetic clip (desk, laptop, library, gym), with her as a cutout in the lower left still talking. A new visual every 1-2 s without losing her face.
- **Captions on two levels:** small white sans word-by-word at chest height, plus a big white italic serif for the 1-3 key words ("so let me EXPLAIN", "on your OWN", "you already KNOW"), with small sparkle stars.
- **One meme beat** (SpongeBob "hmmm") on the line about waiting for someone to figure it out for us.
- **Ending on a rule people repost:** "Never abandon your own foundation to be loved by someone else," over sunset silhouettes. The last line is the caption-able quote.
- Same setup as Elena: seated, one take, dark top. The lift comes from the b-roll cutout layer and the study scans.

## Post-mortem: "Black Friday as you know it is over" (Nov 27 2026), performed poorly
Our read (hypotheses until we see her retention graph):
- **Inside-baseball premise.** The story was an industry fight (agentic commerce, ad models, Amazon-Meta talks) about a behavior most viewers don't have: only ~7% start shopping with AI. Nothing in it touched what the viewer does today.
- **The hook promised something the video didn't deliver.** "Black Friday is over" plus two CEO faces suggests drama or deals; the body was a policy explainer.
- **Too many threads.** About 12 beats (credentials, subway ad, chart, Gemini story, Amazon's own bot, Walmart, Google/Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic, the talks, the "new manipulation", advice). Each revision round added facts to be "maximally informative"; none cut.
- **A hedged payoff.** The twist rested on "reportedly" and "might", so it landed soft.
- **Too long for the payload:** 3:01, her longest. Her best (Venn) was shorter and had one clear verdict.
- **Visually flat:** one static late-night shot in bed and 4 graphics in 3 minutes, against the ~3 s visual-change learning. ("Simplest version" was her call on a tired night; still a factor.)
- **Timing:** posted on Black Friday itself, when the audience is shopping, not watching explainers about shopping.
- Claude recommended this topic (scored it 13/14). The scoring missed "does the viewer already do this?" and "can it be one sentence?".

## Red flags: stop and say so when a new idea shows these
1. **The viewer doesn't already do the thing.** If the story is about a behavior most people don't have yet (AI agents shopping, a niche tool), it needs a personal stake in the first 5 s or it's a pass.
2. **It can't be said in one sentence with one villain or one reversal.** "Amazon blocks bots but has its own" is one; "the AI shopping ecosystem's ad incentives" is not.
3. **More than ~6 beats, or the script grows past ~220 words / ~90 s for a news take.** Each revision should cut a beat for every fact it adds.
4. **The payoff depends on "reportedly", "might", or a forecast.** Fine as a detail; never as the twist.
5. **The hook names a big claim the body doesn't prove fast.** The proof has to land within ~15 s.
6. **A long single shot with fewer than one visual change per ~10 s.**
7. **Competing with the event itself** (posting a think-piece during the event when people are busy doing it). Post the day before or the day after.

## @interval_news "178 websites hit in cyberattack" (Nov 2026), the house style going forward
76 s, ~15K likes, 126 comments. Elena wants to copy the look and pace, with her own dry humor on top.
- **Hook = big number + direct stakes + a promise:** "178 major websites were compromised... Here's a list of the top websites you may have visited and a few critical things you need to do."
- **Utility over opinion:** a list people scan for themselves (did I use Zoom? IKEA?) plus one concrete action (update your phone, check your bank). This is what gets shares ("tell your friends and family").
- **Title card:** left-aligned, white, 2 lines, Helvetica-style grotesk (free match: Inter Tight 500, tight tracking), with a smaller 2-line subtitle under it. No box, no shadow, no color.
- **Captions:** ONE word at a time, small white sans, centered at chest height. Quiet, never competes with the face.
- **Evidence as the graphic:** full-width screenshots of the actual source article (Newsweek) and screen recordings scrolling the real list on a phone. No illustrations, no animation. That's where the trust comes from.
- **Set:** dark, warm practical light, podcast mic in frame, centered medium close-up, eye contact. Looks like a news desk without trying.
- **Plain delivery:** short declarative sentences, no jokes; the facts carry it. Elena's version adds one dry line per beat, not more.
- **Length ~75 s, ~180 words.**

## @erinmerylstudy "words you've never heard" (Nov 2026)
87 s, ~3K likes, 112 comments. Elena loves the scripting and editing.
- **Hook = an expert claim plus a promise:** "Linguists argue that the limits of our vocabulary are the limits of our understanding of the world. So here are some very interesting words... you've probably never come across."
- **The ending calls back to that opening claim** ("expand your description... expand your perception"), so it feels finished. The CTA "let me know if you want a part two" drives comments; posting the part two is still a risk, per Matilde.
- **Fixed rhythm:** 15 items, about 5 s each, same pattern every time: the word, then a one-line definition. Easy to follow, saveable.
- **Her cutout stays on screen in front of a full-bleed mood image** that changes with every item (fire for limerence, a crowd for sonder, rain-soaked forest for petrichor). The background changes every ~5 s and her face never leaves. A dictionary card (the word as a headline, the definition, one phrase highlighted yellow) sits at chest height; small bold captions sit below.
- Calm, even delivery with expressive hands. No jokes needed; the images carry the mood.
- **For Elena:** (1) keep her cutout in front of the evidence and backdrops on list beats instead of cutting away; (2) a dictionary card for jargon (zero-day, patch, pre-positioning); (3) bookend: the last line answers the first; (4) series idea: "tech words you'll hear this year and what they mean," rotating backdrops.
